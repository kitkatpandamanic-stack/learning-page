# Learning experience: PandaDev vs the evidence

Compared 2026-10-16 at repo HEAD `e5e43cb` by the learning-architect agent. Read-only run: nothing in `content/` or `src/` was changed. Research basis: [research/summary.md](../research/summary.md) ("Lesson, exercise, quiz and motivation design rules", "Skills beyond the language"), [research/learning-science.md](../research/learning-science.md) and the project and exercise-style parts of [research/courses.md](../research/courses.md). Citations: `[summary #N]` is row N of the design-rules table, `[learning-science Sn]` and `[courses Sn]` are source rows.

**Verdict**

1. The base is strong. 286 of 288 lessons have an auto-checked exercise, all 491 quizzes explain their answer, and the daily review is real spaced retrieval (Leitner boxes at 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days) that shows the explanation after each answer.
2. Weakest point: help and feedback inside lessons. 275 exercises have exactly one hint, and that hint usually gives most of the code. The solution can be opened at any time, and passing after opening it pays the same XP. A failed test shows its name with a ✗, and no expected-vs-got.
3. The quizzes have cues that let learners guess. The correct answer is option 2 in 336 of 491 quizzes (68%) and the longest option in 316 (64%). Options never shuffle, in lessons or in review.
4. Reading and tracing come before writing only by accident. Every `CodeExample` shows its output up front. No lesson uses Parsons problems or labelled worked steps, and L0 lessons have no diagrams.
5. Motivation is low-stakes and fair: the leaderboard and reminders are opt-in, freezes apply themselves, and a broken streak gets no blame. But 57% of course XP (7,160 of 12,475) is paid for pressing "Complete lesson", whatever the learner actually did.

## Review and memory

- **Is there spaced retrieval?** Yes. `/review` re-asks quiz questions from lessons the learner has marked complete, on a Leitner schedule. A right answer moves the card up a box (1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60 days), and a wrong one sends it back to box 0, due tomorrow (`src/lib/review-schedule.ts` `nextReview`). Answers are graded on the server (`answerReview` in `src/lib/learning.ts`). Due cards come first, oldest and shakiest first, then up to 5 new ones a day, 10 a sitting. The review is done when nothing is waiting or 10 have been answered, and that pays 15 XP once a day. This matches rule 2 [learning-science S30, S31, S32].
- **Does it include feedback?** Yes. After each answer the learner sees "Not quite. The answer is …" or "Right!", the quiz's explanation, and a link back to the lesson (`src/components/learning/review-session.tsx`, `messages/en/review.json`). That is the "feedback after the answer" that roughly doubles the testing effect [learning-science S28, S29].
- **Gaps:**
  1. Only multiple choice. Exercises and practice problems never come back, so the deck never asks the learner to write code from memory.
  2. The options come in the same order as in the lesson, and 68% of answers are option 2. On a second pass a learner can remember the position instead of the idea.
  3. Cards come only from lessons the learner marked complete (`finishedPages`). Learners who skip ahead, often experts, get no review of what they skipped.
  4. No confidence rating, so confidently held wrong models stay hidden [learning-science S47].
  5. Look-alike items aren't paired on purpose [learning-science S33].
  6. Saved code (`src/lib/saved-code.ts`, `src/lib/code-sync.ts`) brings back the learner's old attempt on another device, which is convenient. But reopening an exercise shows their last answer rather than a fresh try.

## Beginners vs experts

- **Placement:** `/welcome` asks "How much have you coded before?" with three self-reported options, 0, 1 and 2 (`messages/en/welcome.json`, `src/lib/welcome.ts`). It sets where "Continue" starts (`startLesson` in `src/lib/learning.ts`). There's no placement test, no Level 3 start, and nothing per module. Rapid first-step tests are the evidence-backed way to place learners [learning-science S9].
- **Skipping ahead:** every lesson is open; no lock was found in `src/`. An expert can jump anywhere, but gets no signal about what's safe to skip, no "test out of this module", and no review cards for what they skipped.
- **Scaffolding that fades:** it does the reverse. L0 has the most blank editors (12) and the smallest share of the solution in the starter (29%). L3 hands over the most (69%), often whole helper files and test suites, as in `javascript/system-design/03-code-review-and-leadership` and `python/production-service/03-ship-it`. Some of that is realistic: seniors work in existing codebases. But L0 learners get the blank page before they've done a single modify task. Expertise reversal says to fade guidance as skill grows [learning-science S8].
- **Within a lesson:** support is the same for everyone. Every exercise shows the same hint and solution, with no "skip the walkthrough, go straight to the task" path. Lessons-learned already notes that the 25-minute `duration` cap understates long L2–3 lessons (`site.md`, open question 3).

## Strengths

- **Real, checked practice in every lesson.** 286 of 288 lessons have an exercise that runs in the browser with no install, in JS, TS (strict), Python, PostgreSQL or React. The two without one are `javascript/modules-and-tooling/02-npm-and-git` and `python/modules-and-packages/02-pip-and-virtual-environments`.
- **Checks that can't be faked.** SQL `rerun()` changes the data and runs the learner's query again (`sql/first-queries/03-filtering-with-where`). Python `_panda_pytest(patch=…)` makes learners' own tests catch four buggy versions (`python/testing/01-first-tests`). JS review tasks run a real vitest suite (`javascript/system-design/03-code-review-and-leadership`). That is feedback on understanding, not on output matching.
- **Misconceptions taught head-on.** Wrong-join demos, `scores = scores.sort()`, aliasing, an `async` function returning a Promise, serial `await`s, AND before OR. 18 lessons have a "Common mistakes"-style heading, and the distractors in the sampled quizzes are real wrong models.
- **Bug-hunt and fix-it exercises** (`javascript/errors-and-debugging/01-reading-errors`, `sql/security/02-sql-injection`) give the modify step the research recommends.
- **Spaced review with explanations**, plus Telegram quiz polls that reach learners outside the site.
- **Practice hints done right.** 160 of 160 problems have 3 graduated hints ending in a skeleton, and every problem links to a refresher lesson.
- **Humane gamification.** The leaderboard and reminders are opt-in, freezes work by themselves, a broken streak isn't shamed and the best streak stays visible. Quiz XP pays only a right first try, and the daily goal is the learner's own choice.
- **Real-world L2–3 content:** design docs, blameless postmortems, review etiquette with labelled nits, zero-downtime migrations, RLS.
- **Full EN and RU parity**, enforced by the build (`site.md`).

## Recommendations

Ranked by expected learning gain for the effort. "Content-only" is work in `content/**` that this team can do. "Needs the infra session" is app code that another Claude session owns. Everything here is free to run.

1. **Remove the quiz answer cues.** Shuffle the options when they're shown, in lessons and in review, keyed by the original index so grading and review cards don't change (needs the infra session, S). Then trim correct options that are clearly longer than the rest (content-only, M; 190 quizzes are 30% or more longer, and EN and RU must change together). Why: retrieval only helps when the learner has to recall the answer [learning-science S28, S29], and distractors should be the thing that tests the model [summary #11]. The harm from the cues is my inference. Effort: S + M.
2. **Make lesson hints graduated.** Split each lesson's single hint into 2–3 steps (nudge → concept → partial code), the way practice problems already do, and add hints to the 9 exercises that have none. Start with L0–1 (128 exercises). Why: [summary #10], [learning-science S20]. Content-only, M–L. `HintSteps` already supports several hints.
3. **Predict, then run, at L0–1.** In each L0–1 lesson, turn one `CodeExample` into a `TryIt` with "What will this print? Decide, then press Run". A `TryIt` without `html` doesn't run by itself (`src/components/mdx/try-it.tsx`) (content-only, M). Optionally, a `predict` mode on `CodeExample` that hides the output until clicked (needs the infra session, S). Don't move existing quizzes earlier: quiz ids are positional (`lessons-learned.md`). Why: [summary #5], [learning-science S11, S12, S15].
4. **Feedback that says what to try next.** Write test names as the expectation plus where to look, for example "Doesn't change the original list (`.sort()` changes it in place)" (content-only, M). Show expected vs got for failing `tests`, not only for `expectedOutput` (needs the infra session, M). Why: [summary #3], [learning-science S17, S18].
5. **Pay XP for learning, not clicks.** Pay lesson XP only once the exercise is solved or attempted, or turn it into a progress marker. Pay less exercise XP, or none, after "Show solution" has been opened, and say so next to the button. Count streak days from exercises, quizzes or review, not from Complete alone (needs the infra session, S–M). Why: [summary #15], [learning-science S20, S38].
6. **Placement and "already know this?"** Add a short "Already know this?" `Callout` at the start of each L1–2 module: what it covers, plus a link to its mini-project or a matching practice problem to test out on (content-only, S–M). Add a 5–8 question predict-style placement check and a Level 3 start to `/welcome` (needs the infra session, M). Why: [summary #13], [learning-science S8, S9].
7. **Mental models at L0–1.** Add simple box-and-arrow ASCII diagrams, the style JS L2 already uses, for assignment, list and object references, the call stack in recursion, and SQL's logical order. Pair each with a quiz that a wrong model gets wrong, placed after the existing quizzes (content-only, M). Why: [summary #7], [learning-science S13, S42, S44].
8. **Use → modify → create at L0.** Give the 12 blank L0 starters a partly written program to change, and keep the blank editor for the module's mini-project (content-only, S). Add a Parsons exercise type, lines to put in order, between reading and writing (needs the infra session, M). Why: [summary #6, #9], [learning-science S2, S4, S5].
9. **A stronger review.** Interleave look-alike cards on purpose, add an optional "How sure are you?" tap, add short code-recall cards from finished exercises, and offer review of skipped lessons to learners who placed above Level 0 (needs the infra session, M). Why: [summary #11, #12], [learning-science S33, S47].
10. **Gentler streak messages and a fairer daily problem.** Reword the Telegram reminder around progress ("You're on a 6-day run, a 5-minute review keeps it going") and drop "save it for a day you really need it". Let a broken streak be earned back, for example by finishing the next day's review. Pick the problem of the day from difficulties that match the learner's level (needs the infra session, S). Why: [summary #15], [learning-science S39, S40].
