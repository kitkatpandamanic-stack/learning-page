The panda shop is giving a golden apron 🐼 to every customer who owns the **complete kitchen set**: they have bought **every product** in the `'kitchen'` category.

Show those customers with the columns `name` and `email`, sorted by `name`.

Some rules make this tricky:

- Only orders that were **not cancelled** count.
- Buying the **same product twice** doesn't make up for a missing one.
- The shop will add new kitchen products, so **don't hard-code** how many there are: with a new kitchen product, nobody owns the full set until they buy it too.

```sql
-- name       | email
-- Ravi Patel | ravi@pandadev.test
```

Keep the customers whose number of different kitchen products matches the size of the whole kitchen range.

<Hint>

Think of it as counting: a customer owns the full set when the number of **different** kitchen products they bought equals the number of kitchen products in the shop. Where can that second number come from without hard-coding it?

</Hint>

<Hint>

Join `customers` to `orders`, `order_items` and `products`, keeping only orders that aren't cancelled and products in the `'kitchen'` category. `GROUP BY c.id, c.name, c.email`. In `HAVING`, compare `count(DISTINCT p.id)` with a subquery, `(SELECT count(*) FROM products WHERE category = 'kitchen')`. `DISTINCT` stops repeat purchases counting twice, and the subquery keeps up when new kitchen products arrive.

</Hint>

<Hint>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT c.name, c.email
FROM customers AS c
JOIN orders AS o
  ON o.customer_id = c.id AND o.status <> 'cancelled'
JOIN order_items AS oi ON oi.order_id = o.id
JOIN products AS p
  ON p.id = oi.product_id AND p.category = 'kitchen'
GROUP BY c.id, c.name, c.email
HAVING count(DISTINCT p.id) =
  (SELECT count(*) FROM products WHERE category = 'kitchen')
ORDER BY c.name;
```

The "double negative" works too: keep a customer when `NOT EXISTS` a kitchen product for which `NOT EXISTS` a non-cancelled order line of theirs.

</Solution>
