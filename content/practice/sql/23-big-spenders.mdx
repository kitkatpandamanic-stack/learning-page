The panda shop wants to thank its **big spenders** with a handwritten card 💌. A big spender is a customer who spent **more than the average customer**.

Count only orders that **weren't cancelled**, and work out the average over the customers who **have spent something** (customers with no orders, or only cancelled ones, aren't part of the average).

Show:

- `name`: the customer's name,
- `orders`: how many non-cancelled orders they placed,
- `spent`: the total of those orders (quantity × unit price, summed).

Put the **biggest spender first**; if two customers spent the same, sort them by name.

```sql
-- name       | orders | spent
-- Ravi Patel |      3 | 282.23
-- Sam Ortiz  |      2 | 273.83
-- …
```

Finish the query in three steps: order totals, customer totals, and the customers above the average.

<Hint>

Filter out cancelled orders in `order_totals`. Add a second CTE, `customer_totals`, that groups `order_totals` by `customer_id` with `count(*)` and `sum(total)`. In the final `SELECT`, join `customers` for the name and compare with a scalar subquery: `WHERE spent > (SELECT avg(spent) FROM customer_totals)`.

</Hint>

<Solution>

```sql
WITH order_totals AS (
  SELECT o.id, o.customer_id,
    sum(oi.quantity * oi.unit_price) AS total
  FROM orders AS o
  JOIN order_items AS oi ON oi.order_id = o.id
  WHERE o.status <> 'cancelled'
  GROUP BY o.id
),
customer_totals AS (
  SELECT customer_id, count(*) AS orders, sum(total) AS spent
  FROM order_totals
  GROUP BY customer_id
)
SELECT c.name, ct.orders, ct.spent
FROM customer_totals AS ct
JOIN customers AS c ON c.id = ct.customer_id
WHERE ct.spent > (SELECT avg(spent) FROM customer_totals)
ORDER BY ct.spent DESC, c.name;
```
