The finance team wants a one-page payroll summary 📊. For each department that has staff, show:

- **`department`**: the department's name (from `departments`);
- **`headcount`**: how many employees work there;
- **`avg_salary`**: the average salary as a **whole number, rounded down** (so 91666.67 becomes 91666);
- **`pct_of_payroll`**: the department's salaries as a **percentage of the whole company's payroll**, **rounded to 1 decimal place**.

The whole payroll includes **everyone**, the CEO too (she has no department), so the percentages add up to a bit less than 100. Sort by **`pct_of_payroll`, biggest first**, then by department name.

```sql
-- department  | headcount | avg_salary | pct_of_payroll
-- Engineering | 6         | 91666      | 34.8
-- …
```

Remember: dividing one whole number by another in PostgreSQL throws away the fraction, so `100 * 550000 / 1580500` is `34`, not `34.8`.

Add the average and the percentage, taking care with whole-number division.

<Hint>

`sum(salary) / count(*)` divides whole numbers, which rounds down for you; `floor(avg(salary))` does the same. For the percentage, get the company total with a subquery, `(SELECT sum(salary) FROM employees)`, and start with `100.0 * …` so the division keeps its decimals before `round(…, 1)`.

</Hint>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT
  d.name AS department,
  count(*) AS headcount,
  sum(e.salary) / count(*) AS avg_salary,
  round(100.0 * sum(e.salary) / (SELECT sum(salary) FROM employees), 1) AS pct_of_payroll
FROM employees AS e
JOIN departments AS d ON d.id = e.department_id
GROUP BY d.name
ORDER BY pct_of_payroll DESC, department;
```

</Solution>

`sum(sum(e.salary)) OVER ()` would give the total of the departments only, which leaves out the CEO, so the subquery is the right total here.

</Exercise>
