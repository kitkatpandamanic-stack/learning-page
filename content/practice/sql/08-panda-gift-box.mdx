The panda shop is putting together a gift box 🎁. A product can go in the box when **all** of these are true:

- it's **on theme**: its name contains **"bamboo"** or **"panda"** (in **any** upper or lower case), **or** it's in the **`tea`** category;
- it costs **from 3 to 20**, both ends included;
- it's **in stock** (`stock` above 0);
- it's **not** in the **`kitchen`** category (pots and chopsticks break in the post).

Show the **`name`**, **`category`** and **`price`** of the products that qualify, **most expensive first**, then by name.

```sql
-- name          | category | price
-- Plush Panda   | toys     | 18.00
-- Matcha Powder | tea      | 14.00
-- …
```

Watch out: `AND` is evaluated before `OR`, so the "on theme" part needs brackets.

<Exercise
  language="sql"
  title="Panda gift box"
  starter={`-- name, category and price of gift-box products,
-- most expensive first
SELECT name, category, price
FROM products
WHERE name LIKE '%Bamboo%'
ORDER BY price DESC, name;
`}
  tests={[
    { name: "Columns are name, category, price", check: "columns.join() === 'name,category,price'" },
    { name: "The right products, most expensive first", check: "rows.map(r => r.name).join() === 'Plush Panda,Matcha Powder,The Bamboo Code,Jasmine Tea,Panda Cookies'" },
    { name: "Bamboo Crisps (2.99) is too cheap", check: "!rows.some(r => r.name === 'Bamboo Crisps')" },
    { name: "Earl Grey is out of stock and the chopsticks are kitchen items", check: "!rows.some(r => r.name === 'Earl Grey' || r.name === 'Bamboo Chopsticks')" },
    { name: "Works when the data changes", check: "(r => r.map(x => x.name).join() === 'Plush Panda,Matcha Powder,The Bamboo Code,Jasmine Tea,Earl Grey,Panda Cookies,bamboo straws')(await rerun(\"INSERT INTO products (name, category, price, stock) VALUES ('bamboo straws', 'home', 3.00, 50), ('Panda Apron', 'kitchen', 9.00, 10); UPDATE products SET stock = 10 WHERE name = 'Earl Grey'\"))" },
  ]}
>

Combine the four rules so that only real gift-box products are left.

<Hint>

Write the four rules down one by one, then think about how they fit together. Remember that `AND` binds tighter than `OR`, and that `LIKE` cares about upper and lower case.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Turn each rule into a condition:

- on theme: `name ILIKE '%bamboo%' OR name ILIKE '%panda%' OR category = 'tea'`, all wrapped in brackets;
- price: `price BETWEEN 3 AND 20`, which includes both ends;
- in stock: `stock > 0`;
- not kitchen: `category <> 'kitchen'` or `category NOT IN ('kitchen')`.

Join the last three to the bracketed part with `AND`, and keep `ORDER BY price DESC, name`.

</Hint>

<Hint>

```sql
SELECT name, category, price
FROM products
WHERE (name ILIKE '%bamboo%' OR ... OR ...)
  AND price BETWEEN 3 AND 20
  AND ...  -- in stock and not kitchen
ORDER BY price DESC, name;
```

</Hint>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT name, category, price
FROM products
WHERE (name ILIKE '%bamboo%' OR name ILIKE '%panda%' OR category = 'tea')
  AND price BETWEEN 3 AND 20
  AND stock > 0
  AND category NOT IN ('kitchen')
ORDER BY price DESC, name;
```

</Solution>

Without the brackets, `category = 'tea' AND price BETWEEN …` would be grouped together, and every bamboo product would sneak in whatever its price.

</Exercise>
