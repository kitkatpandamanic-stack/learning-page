Life in London got pricier, so the panda company is giving its London staff a rise 💷. Every employee who works in a department **located in London** (see the `location` column of `departments`) and earns **less than 80000** gets **4% more**, **rounded to the nearest 100**.

Write one `UPDATE` on `employees` that ends with `RETURNING name, salary`, so you see each changed person with their **new** salary. The order of the returned rows doesn't matter.

```sql
-- name              | salary
-- Linus Park        |  74900   -- was 72000: 72000 × 1.04 = 74880 → 74900
-- Margaret Hamilton |  79000
-- …
```

Find the departments by their location, not by typing their ids: if the Sales team moves to London next year, the same statement should include them.

Write the `UPDATE` that gives the London staff under 80000 their rise, returning `name` and `salary`.

<Hint>

The location lives in `departments`, but you're changing `employees`. In PostgreSQL an `UPDATE` can read another table through a `FROM` clause. And how do you round to the nearest hundred?

</Hint>

<Hint>

Write `UPDATE employees AS e SET … FROM departments AS d WHERE d.id = e.department_id AND …`. Add the two conditions: `d.location = 'London'` and `e.salary < 80000`. The new salary is `e.salary * 1.04`, rounded with `round(x, -2)` (a negative number of digits rounds to the left of the decimal point). `RETURNING` shows the values after the change.

</Hint>

<Hint>

```sql
UPDATE employees AS e
SET salary = ...  -- 4% more, to the nearest 100
FROM departments AS d
WHERE d.id = e.department_id
  AND ...  -- in London, under 80000
RETURNING e.name, e.salary;
```

</Hint>

<Solution>

```sql
UPDATE employees AS e
SET salary = round(e.salary * 1.04, -2)
FROM departments AS d
WHERE d.id = e.department_id
  AND d.location = 'London'
  AND e.salary < 80000
RETURNING e.name, e.salary;
```

A subquery works too: `WHERE salary < 80000 AND department_id IN (SELECT id FROM departments WHERE location = 'London')`. Grace Hopper has no department at all, so neither version touches her.

</Solution>
