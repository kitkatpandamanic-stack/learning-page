HR wants to see each department's **pay ladder** 🪜. For every employee who **belongs to a department**, show:

- `department`: the department's name,
- `name`: the employee's name,
- `salary`: their salary,
- `pay_rank`: their place in the department by salary, highest first. Equal salaries **share a place**, and the next place **isn't skipped** (1, 2, 2, 3),
- `behind_top`: how much less they earn than the best-paid person in their department (0 for the top earner).

Sort by department, then `pay_rank`, then name.

```sql
-- department | name        | salary | pay_rank | behind_top
-- Design     | Don Norman  |  95000 |        1 |          0
-- Design     | Susan Kare  |  64000 |        2 |      31000
-- …
```

SELECT d.name AS department, e.name, e.salary

ORDER BY department, e.salary DESC;

>

Add the two window columns, `pay_rank` and `behind_top`.

<Hint>

Both columns use a window **per department**: `OVER (PARTITION BY e.department_id …)`. For the place, `dense_rank()` with `ORDER BY e.salary DESC` gives ties the same number without gaps. For the gap, subtract the salary from `max(e.salary) OVER (PARTITION BY e.department_id)`.

</Hint>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT d.name AS department, e.name, e.salary,
  dense_rank() OVER (PARTITION BY e.department_id ORDER BY e.salary DESC) AS pay_rank,
  max(e.salary) OVER (PARTITION BY e.department_id) - e.salary AS behind_top
FROM employees AS e
JOIN departments AS d ON d.id = e.department_id
ORDER BY department, pay_rank, e.name;
```

</Solution>
