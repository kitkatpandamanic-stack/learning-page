The panda company's HR team is reviewing pay 💼. For **every** employee, show who they report to and how much more their manager earns:

- `employee`: the employee's name,
- `manager`: their manager's name,
- `pay_gap`: the manager's `salary` minus the employee's `salary`.

The CEO has no manager, but she **must still appear**, with NULL in both `manager` and `pay_gap`.

Sort by `pay_gap`, **biggest first**, and put the CEO's row **at the very end**. When two gaps are equal, sort by `employee`.

```sql
-- employee     | manager      | pay_gap
-- Hana Sato    | Grace Hopper |   80000
-- Mary Kay     | Grace Hopper |   60000
-- …
-- Grace Hopper | NULL         |    NULL
```

Join `employees` to itself to find each manager, and keep the CEO.

<Hint>

Each manager is also a row in `employees`. How can you join a table to itself? And which kind of join keeps the CEO, who has no manager?

</Hint>

<Hint>

Use `employees` twice with two aliases: `e` for the employee and `m` for the manager, joined `ON m.id = e.manager_id`. Make it a `LEFT JOIN` so the CEO stays, with NULL for `m.name` and for `m.salary - e.salary`. In PostgreSQL `DESC` puts NULLs first, so sort with `pay_gap DESC NULLS LAST, employee`.

</Hint>

<Hint>

```sql
SELECT e.name AS employee,
  m.name AS manager,
  ... AS pay_gap
FROM employees AS e
LEFT JOIN employees AS m ON ...
ORDER BY pay_gap DESC ..., employee;  -- the CEO last
```

</Hint>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT e.name AS employee,
  m.name AS manager,
  m.salary - e.salary AS pay_gap
FROM employees AS e
LEFT JOIN employees AS m ON m.id = e.manager_id
ORDER BY pay_gap DESC NULLS LAST, employee;
```

</Solution>
