## Meet the catalogue

Good analysts look at the data before answering questions about it. How many films are there, and which years do they cover? Then peek at a few rows to see what each column holds:

## Question 1: What are our best films?

The organiser's first question is easy: *"Which five films have the highest ratings?"* Sort by `rating` from highest to lowest with `DESC`, then keep the first five rows with `LIMIT`:

<CodeExample output={`         title          | rating
------------------------+--------
 The Quiet Algorithm    |    8.4
 Midnight in the Forest |    8.1
 Mountain of Echoes     |    7.9
 The Bamboo Heist       |    7.8
 Snow Leopard Summer    |    7.6
(5 rows)`}>

```sql
SELECT title, rating
FROM movies
ORDER BY rating DESC, title
LIMIT 5;
```

</CodeExample>

Notice the **second sort key**, `title`. No two of these five films share a rating today, but if two films ever did, the database could list them in either order. Adding `title` as a tie-breaker makes the answer the same every time.

## Question 2: Something short for a weeknight

*"Some Fridays we start late. Which films are under 100 minutes?"* That's a `WHERE` filter with a comparison:

<CodeExample output={`         title         | minutes
-----------------------+---------
 The Great Bamboo Race |      88
 Tea for Two Thousand  |      93
 The Last Dumpling     |      95
 Snow Leopard Summer   |      98
(4 rows)`}>

```sql
SELECT title, minutes
FROM movies
WHERE minutes < 100
ORDER BY minutes, title;
```

</CodeExample>

`<` means **less than**, so a film of exactly 100 minutes would not be included. Use `<=` when the limit itself should count.

## Question 4: Searching by title

A member remembers *"that film with bamboo in the name"*, but not whether it was "Bamboo" or "bamboo". `ILIKE` matches a pattern **ignoring case**, and `%` stands for "any text, or none":

<CodeExample output={`         title         | year
-----------------------+------
 The Great Bamboo Race | 2014
 The Bamboo Heist      | 2019
(2 rows)`}>

```sql
SELECT title, year
FROM movies
WHERE title ILIKE '%bamboo%'
ORDER BY year;
```

</CodeExample>

`'%bamboo%'` means "anything, then *bamboo*, then anything", so the word can appear anywhere in the title. With `LIKE` instead of `ILIKE`, the lower-case pattern would find nothing, because both titles spell it with a capital B.

## Question 5: Comedies and dramas from before 2020

Combining conditions needs care. Here's a first attempt and a fixed one:

<CodeExample output={`         title          | genre  | year
------------------------+--------+------
 Midnight in the Forest | drama  | 2015
 The Bamboo Heist       | comedy | 2019
 The Last Dumpling      | comedy | 2021
(3 rows)

         title          | genre  | year
------------------------+--------+------
 Midnight in the Forest | drama  | 2015
 The Bamboo Heist       | comedy | 2019
(2 rows)`}>

```sql
-- Oops: AND is checked before OR
SELECT title, genre, year
FROM movies
WHERE genre = 'comedy' OR genre = 'drama' AND year < 2020
ORDER BY year;

-- Fixed: the genre test is grouped first
SELECT title, genre, year
FROM movies
WHERE genre IN ('comedy', 'drama') AND year < 2020
ORDER BY year;
```

</CodeExample>

The first query sneaks in *The Last Dumpling* from 2021. SQL reads `AND` **before** `OR`, just like maths does `×` before `+`, so the database understood it as *"any comedy, or a drama from before 2020"*. The fix groups the two genres with `IN`. Brackets work too: `(genre = 'comedy' OR genre = 'drama') AND year < 2020`.

<Callout type="warning" title="Mixing AND and OR">

Whenever a `WHERE` has both `AND` and `OR`, add brackets (or use `IN`) so the meaning is obvious. It costs nothing and saves you from answers that look right but quietly include the wrong rows.

</Callout>

## Question 6: The catalogue, page by page

The club wants to print the catalogue for its noticeboard, four films per page, in title order. `LIMIT` sets the page size and `OFFSET` skips the earlier pages. Page 2 skips the first 4 films:

<CodeExample output={`         title         | year
-----------------------+------
 Ninja Pandas II       | 2022
 Panda Express to Mars | 2023
 Snow Leopard Summer   | 2020
 Tea for Two Thousand  | 2017
(4 rows)`}>

```sql
SELECT title, year
FROM movies
ORDER BY title
LIMIT 4 OFFSET 4;
```

</CodeExample>

For page `n`, the offset is `(n - 1) * 4`: page 1 uses `OFFSET 0`, page 2 `OFFSET 4`, page 3 `OFFSET 8`.

The club votes on Friday's film from a **shortlist**. Write the query that builds it. A film makes the shortlist when:

- its `rating` is **7 or more**,
- it runs **110 minutes or less**,
- and it is **not** a `thriller` (a member can't stand them).

Show the columns `title`, `genre`, `rating` and `minutes`, with the **highest rating first**. If two films share a rating, sort them by **title**. With today's catalogue you should get three films, starting with *The Bamboo Heist*.

<Hint>

You need three conditions joined with `AND`: `rating >= 7`, `minutes <= 110` and `genre <> 'thriller'` (`<>` means "not equal"). Then sort with `ORDER BY rating DESC, title`.

</Hint>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT title, genre, rating, minutes
FROM movies
WHERE rating >= 7
  AND minutes <= 110
  AND genre <> 'thriller'
ORDER BY rating DESC, title;
```

## Recap

- Start every analysis by **exploring**: count the rows, check the ranges with `min` and `max`, and peek at a few rows.
- `ORDER BY … DESC` with `LIMIT` answers "top N" questions; add a **tie-breaker** (like `title`) so the order never changes.
- `WHERE` filters rows with comparisons (`<`, `<=`, `<>`), `IN`, and `ILIKE` patterns with `%`.
- `DISTINCT` lists each value once.
- `AND` is checked before `OR`: use brackets or `IN` when you mix them.
- `LIMIT 4 OFFSET (n - 1) * 4` shows page `n` of a list.
